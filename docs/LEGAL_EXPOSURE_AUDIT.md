## Launch blockers for Kazakhstan schools

Kazakhstan's Personal Data Law requires an appropriate collection/processing basis, addresses consent by a subject or legal representative, requires local storage, and regulates cross-border transfers. See [official law, articles 7, 8, 12 and 16](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/Z1300000094) and the [government-published law text](https://www.gov.kz/uploads/2021/7/12/8bd1ab6ec99fb5448ac343b0611e4829_original.110350.pdf). The latter is an older published copy; the current consolidated law and implementation rules must govern launch review.

The current Render/Neon/R2/Cloudflare arrangement has **not** been verified as meeting Kazakhstan storage/transfer requirements. Neither a consent checkbox, an age screen, an operator flag nor self-hosted fonts resolves that issue. No infrastructure configuration or data location was changed. Before real-school rollout:

- Identify the actual operator (an individual or legal entity), service contacts, purposes, data categories, recipients, retention/deletion process and applicable school/operator responsibilities. Prepare accurate Russian/Kazakh notices and agreements with Kazakhstan counsel; no invented operator or legal promise is published here.
- Verify guardian authority and the legal basis for each minor's processing before creating their identifiable account or accepting their work. A teacher's click or parental-email checkbox is not proof. Establish withdrawal, correction/deletion handling and evidence retention. Existing student data also needs review. This requires a real consent/authorization workflow and school agreements; it remains unresolved.
- Verify Kazakhstan locations for the primary database, originals, backups and any other required storage; review cross-border processing, delivery, logs, support and AI subprocessors. Plan any move separately with verified providers and a reversible migration; do not move production automatically.
- Keep paid AI disabled for real pupil data until lawful transfer, provider/endpoint privacy and approved educational processing have been verified. Existing budget and publication protections are unchanged.

For U.S. exposure, [FTC COPPA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions) distinguishes general-audience, mixed-audience and child-directed services. A child-directed service cannot solve COPPA by simply excluding under-13s. This patch is therefore **not** described as COPPA compliance or approval for child-directed U.S. use.

## Features that must stay absent until prepared

If marketing is introduced, implement genuine suppression/opt-out enforcement before send, a working no-login unsubscribe mechanism, consent/legal-basis records and a valid sender postal address. Under the [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), a registered PO box or qualifying private mailbox can serve as the marketing postal address; an office is not necessary. Kazakhstan advertising/communications requirements must also be reviewed. Do not add promotional copy to the transactional templates.

If paid subscriptions are introduced, show the actual price/currency, billing interval, renewal amount/timing, any trial conversion, commitment and cancellation method/deadline immediately beside purchase controls, obtain explicit informed agreement, and implement the stated cancellation behavior. [ROSCA](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/restore-online-shoppers-confidence-act) is a U.S. reference, not a substitute for Kazakhstan consumer-law review. The vacated 2024 FTC rule is not relied on here.

The new browser policy allows same-origin resources, inline Next/React hydration and styles, and local blob/data previews; it does not claim a nonce-based XSS defense. Development additionally permits HMR WebSockets/eval. Test the authenticated original/PDF/review flows before rollout. Backend must precede frontend because registration now requires an age proof; existing sessions and saved review data remain usable.


## Registration follow-up — age selector and parent mailbox approval

The new slider has no preselected eligibility and requires a deliberate confirmation. Its numeric age remains in page memory; requests contain only `under_13`, `13_to_17` or `18_or_over`. Adult proofs still expire after ten minutes. Stale/mismatched registration proofs return the user to the screen without discarding their name/email; passwords are cleared.

Teen registration uses a signed browser-bound proof and a separate one-use parent permission. No child name, email or password is requested until the parent approves. The parent email and queued link token are encrypted using the existing email key; only a token hash is retained after delivery. Opening a link does not approve anything. Approval requires an explicit adult/guardian attestation and consent against a saved notice version, followed by separate child-email verification. Parent links expire in one hour, resends rotate the token, denial blocks registration, and concurrent registrations cannot consume one approval twice. A parent can withdraw through the live link during its lifetime; afterward the displayed privacy contact handles withdrawal/deletion requests. Revocation closes the child account's sessions and access without deleting academic records. Unused requests are removed by the existing email worker after the expiry/permission window; consumed consent receipts require an operator retention policy.

Prepare additive migration `011_guardian_registration`, release the backend before the frontend, and configure these backend-only variables only after reviewing a real notice and guardian-authority process:

```dotenv
FORMA_GUARDIAN_REGISTRATION_ENABLED=true
FORMA_GUARDIAN_PRIVACY_NOTICE_URL=https://<operator-domain>/<actual-privacy-notice>
FORMA_GUARDIAN_PRIVACY_CONTACT=<public-privacy-contact-email>
```

The feature defaults to disabled and fails closed if the notice/contact or existing transactional-email setup is absent. `FORMA_PUBLIC_REGISTRATION`, SMTP, `FORMA_EMAIL_TOKEN_KEY`, `FRONTEND_ORIGIN` and the existing email worker must also be configured. No production environment, `.env`, real recipients or deployment were changed. A working email path is not certification of guardian authority, consent validity, Kazakhstan residency/transfer compliance or COPPA compliance. The rollout blockers above remain applicable.
